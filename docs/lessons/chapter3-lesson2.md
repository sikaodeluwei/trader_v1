## Confirmation and the qualification of evidence

Slides 2–3 and T01 retain Lesson 1's distinction: three relevant reactions
covering both sides can suggest a potential range; ordinary-range confirmation
requires at least two externally judged unsuccessful upward attempts at
resistance and two unsuccessful downward attempts at support. This count is
objective **conditional on the supplied judgments being valid, distinct and
relevant to the selected boundaries**. An anchor, isolated point or bare touch
does not itself establish an unsuccessful attempt. A relevant near-miss or
failed penetration can qualify without literal contact (slide 2, T02).

Slides 6–7 and T03 sharpen the qualification: the instructor initially says
“我们暂且认为它算” about a second lower reaction, but later says, explicitly
from a retrospective perspective, that it was not effective validation. He
refers to weak rebound strength and later structural/lifecycle interpretation.
This supplies no objective rebound-strength cutoff. It does not authorize
classifying every earlier four-point range as absent whenever it later breaks.
It shows that externally supplied evidence and the identity of the selected
region can be reconsidered once more information exists.

The correction is to any unconditional reading of “four points establish a
range forever.” Lesson 1's actual implementation and documentation already
make confirmation conditional and separate formation evidence from an active
range or lifecycle claim; that contract remains unchanged.

## Boundary drawing, geometry and structural reinterpretation

Slide 8 and T04 teach two principles:

1. **尽量连接、触碰更多的K线。** Use a boundary that represents several relevant
   contacts; it may omit one isolated extreme. In the schematic, shifting the
   upper line captures multiple upper contacts, and the lower line favors
   contacts 1 and 3 over choosing only the deeper point 2.
2. **随着区间不断变化，不断修正上下轨。** Revisit the drawing as new candles
   appear. Slides 4–5 and 9 show an earlier horizontal interpretation followed
   by a sloping interpretation.

These are discretionary drawing principles. “More” supplies neither a maximum
contact objective nor a numeric tolerance, body/wick preference ranking,
lookback, anchor-selection algorithm, parallelism requirement, slope cutoff,
revision trigger or rule for changing a range's lifecycle state.

### Chronological correctness

Keep an earlier judgment tied to the information actually available then.
If a later reaction outcome, boundary choice or structural interpretation
changes, create a separate explicit context with its later true knowledge
time. Reassess evidence against its boundaries and region; do not automatically
carry old event counts across drawings or splits. Do not relabel a prior
snapshot using later candles while presenting it as an earlier classification.

`BoundaryAnchor.index` locates a selected contact, not the time its boundary
was selected. `BoundaryObservation.index` is the time its stated outcome is
known, not the first touch. `known_at_index` dates the complete supplied
annotation, including boundary selection; `None` leaves availability unknown.
Its lower-bound checks cannot independently verify that the analyst really
knew a judgment then. An immutable context is a useful snapshot, not an event
store, revision lineage, historical prefix query or automatic as-of filter.

For example, if a second lower attempt is still uncertain at an earlier time,
it cannot supply confirmation then. If it is externally judged unsuccessful
later, that later context can conditionally confirm ordinary formation. If
subsequent structure leads the analyst to reject that reaction or split the
region, record a later interpretation; preserve the earlier assessment as an
assessment made with its own information. Slides 6–7 do not give exact candle
indexes for when every reinterpretation became knowable.

refute a range; their presence does not replace qualified formation evidence.

### Moving-average interpretation and timeframe

Slide 21's legend identifies EMA20 (the transcript renders it “EM20”). T11
extends the explanation to MA/EMA and single/combined averages, while T12
names 21/55/144. None is an approved universal parameter set or a requirement
that every possible moving average be flat in every range.

Slide 24 intentionally shows a blue range while the averages remain broadly
ordered in a rising background. Slide 25 views the discussed region on a
one-hour chart and shows more convergence/crossing. T13 also describes an
even smaller chart, but the supplied deck has no additional screenshot for
that view. This is a limitation of the screenshot collection, not missing
evidence to reconstruct. Chart timeframe is separate from Chapter 2's
`SHORT`/`MEDIUM`/`LONG` structural hierarchy; no mapping is introduced.

T14's reasoning order matters: noticing entanglement can prompt investigation
of a possible range; qualified range evidence still has to be considered.
Do not start with the desired range label and search selectively for averages
to justify it. This observation-first teaching does not require a moving
average as an input to `evaluate_range_formation`, nor does it make
entanglement sufficient confirmation. Slides 21–22's “均线是失效的” remains the
instructor's contextual warning about directional usefulness, not a universal
empirical result or a rule to disable any system component.
